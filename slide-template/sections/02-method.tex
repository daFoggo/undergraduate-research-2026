\section{Method}

\begin{frame}{Cycle detection: adaptive ON/OFF thresholds}
  \small
  \setlength{\itemsep}{2pt}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \alert{Goal:} find $[t_{\mathrm{start}}, t_{\mathrm{end}}]$ of each
          appliance cycle from raw power, with no hand-tuned thresholds.
    \item \alert{Learn thresholds from data:} pick the power level that best
          separates the OFF (standby) and ON (active) readings\,[1], on
          $\ln(1+P)$ since power is multiplicative and right-skewed\,[4].
    \item \alert{Hysteresis:} two thresholds $P_{\mathrm{on}} > P_{\mathrm{off}}$
          above a noise floor, so a cycle does not flicker around one value.
    \item \alert{Clean up:} drop too-short cycles, merge short pauses\,[2].
          Thresholds directly determine the ON/OFF labels\,[3].
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.15cm}
  \footnotesize
  \parbox{0.86\textwidth}{\alert{Why not Median $+\,k\cdot$MAD?} It assumes OFF dominates, but a fridge
  is ON $\sim$70\% of the time, so the median lands inside ON and cycles are
  missed.}
\end{frame}
